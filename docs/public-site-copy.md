## How it works — `#how-it-works`

**Heading:** A few clear steps toward your next experience.

1. **Start with what works for you.** Explore interests, an age range, availability, and how you can take part. You can be curious without having a career plan.
2. **Look at the requirements together.** Compare age limits, dates, cost, training, and guardian requirements. A reason to explore should explain the facts behind it.
3. **Check the next step.** Before a real application, confirm the details with the organizer. Ask your school separately about any credit or service-hour requirements.

**Prototype note:** This page demonstrates the idea with fictional choices. It cannot submit an application or reserve a place.

The public preview should demonstrate these three steps visibly. Do not describe AI recommendations, verified attendance, automatic applications, badges, a reflection journal, or a completed growth portfolio as available features.

## Families, schools, and organizers — `#families`

**Heading:** Clear details for the people helping you choose.

**Introduction:** Students make better-informed choices when the requirements and the open questions are easy to see. CommonGround is being developed with that clarity in mind.

**For families:** Age limits, travel, supervision, and guardian requirements belong in the conversation before a real activity. A listing or a matching explanation cannot replace those checks.

**For schools:** Whether an activity earns credit or recognized service hours is a separate school decision. CommonGround does not award or guarantee either.

**For organizers:** The planned approach calls for clear participation requirements and permission to use source information. The preview does not imply a partnership or endorsement by any organization.

**Status note:** The local prototype explores private student accounts and guardian or school review with fictional data. A synthetic review does not verify someone's identity, legal authority, or consent. Real minor enrollment remains disabled.

Avoid “parent approved,” “school verified,” “trusted partners,” “vetted opportunities,” or “safe for teens” as badges or general product claims.

## Questions — `#questions`

### Who is CommonGround being built for?

High-school students are the initial focus, including students aged 18 and over. The local teen workflow explores ages 13–17; under-13 accounts are unsupported. Being in high school and being a particular age are separate facts.

### Can I join an opportunity from this page?

This is a prelaunch preview. The activities are fictional and cannot be booked, and this page does not accept applications or account enrollment.

### Are the opportunities real?

All activities shown here are illustrative. Real opportunity feeds are not connected, and the preview does not represent real organizations or available places.

### How would CommonGround decide what fits?

The local prototype checks stated requirements such as age limits, timing, cost, training, and guardian requirements. It then uses selected interests and preferences to explain a possible fit. An explanation helps you compare the facts; the organizer still needs to confirm the details before a real application.

### What does guardian or school review mean?

The prototype lets fictional teen accounts try a review request assigned to a specific adult test account. It does not verify a guardian's identity, a school's authority, or legally approved consent. The real enrollment process still needs an approved policy and safeguarding approach.

### Will an activity count toward school credit or service hours?

Ask your school before applying. Its rules and approval process determine recognition. A saved activity, a match, or a self-reported completion in CommonGround does not guarantee credit or hours.

### Do I need to share personal details to explore?

You can explore this page with fictional choices. There is no early-access form or email signup here. Do not enter real student information into a local test.

### Will CommonGround offer internships, competitions, or college opportunities?

The current focus is helping high-school students explore volunteering and community activities. Other opportunity types and college expansion may be considered later; this preview does not promise their availability.
